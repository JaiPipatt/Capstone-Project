\subsection{Electronics work plan}
\label{sec:elec-work-plan}

The electronics work follows the four project phases, split into A-1, A, B, C
and D. Each phase's block diagram shows the subsystems built in that phase;
dashed boxes mark the module or PDB that holds them.

Phase A-1 uses COTS parts for early locomotion and mechanism testing: a 2S
battery, a 4-channel motor driver with built-in MCU, and an ESP32-C3 for
Wi-Fi (Figure~\ref{fig:phase-a1}).

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{image/Phase_A_1_Diagram.png}
  \caption{Phase A-1 electronics block diagram.}
  \label{fig:phase-a1}
\end{figure}

Phase A needs basic locomotion under external command, and starts the custom
PCB work. A custom PCB is needed because the SMORES package must fit many
components, with onboard perception and compute as the end goal. Two boards
(Figure~\ref{fig:phase-a}):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Power board} --- step-down regulator, LDO, four H-bridge
        drivers with current sensing.
  \item \textbf{Main board} --- MCU for the H-bridges, IMU, UWB for
        inter-module ranging, Wi-Fi.
\end{itemize}

Both boards expose external GPIO for the components added in Phases B, C
and D.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{image/Phase_A_Diagram.png}
  \caption{Phase A electronics block diagram.}
  \label{fig:phase-a}
\end{figure}

Phase B needs module-to-module connection. The work is an in-wheel PCB with
the EP-Face circuitry and the IR contact-alignment circuit. It is powered from
the power board and talks to the main board through a slip ring, covered in
the electrical connection section (Figure~\ref{fig:phase-b}).

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{image/Phase_B_Diagram.png}
  \caption{Phase B electronics block diagram.}
  \label{fig:phase-b}
\end{figure}

Phase C adds onboard perception: an RGB-D/ToF camera, with the compute unit
still wired externally. It also adds a wheel encoder, because the motor's
built-in encoder sits before several gear stages and cannot sense wheel angle
precisely (Figure~\ref{fig:phase-c}).

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{image/Phase_C_Diagram.png}
  \caption{Phase C electronics block diagram.}
  \label{fig:phase-c}
\end{figure}

Phase D moves compute inside the robot: a Raspberry Pi Compute Module 5 on a
custom carrier PCB, connected to the main board and the camera
(Figure~\ref{fig:phase-d}).

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{image/Phase_D_Diagram.png}
  \caption{Phase D electronics block diagram.}
  \label{fig:phase-d}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Additional scope not covered in the proposal: wheel encoder}
\label{sec:wheel-encoder}

The original SMORES sensed joint angle with a continuous-rotation
potentiometer geared to the joint shaft. The wiper voltage gives the shaft
angle directly, so position is absolute at power-up with no homing. The cost
is coarse resolution (about 5$^\circ$), contact wear, and backlash from the
gear stage. The project is replacing this with a non-contact encoder.

Three options were considered; Table~\ref{tab:encoders} compares them.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering\small
\caption{Wheel-rotation sensing options compared.}
\label{tab:encoders}
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.14\textwidth} L{0.26\textwidth} L{0.26\textwidth}
                    L{0.26\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Criterion} &
\textbf{On-axis magnetic rotary encoder (e.g.\ AS5600, AS5048)} &
\textbf{Off-axis magnetic tape encoder (1\,mm pole pitch)} &
\textbf{Custom inductive encoder (PCB coil and inductance-to-digital IC)} \\
\midrule
Sensing principle &
  Diametrically magnetised magnet on the shaft end, read by a Hall-effect IC;
  angle from $\operatorname{atan2}(\sin,\cos)$ &
  Magnetised tape ring bonded to the wheel rim, read off-axis by a
  magnetoresistive or Hall read head at a fixed radial gap &
  PCB transmit and receive coils excite eddy currents in a conductive rotor
  target; phase and amplitude of the return signal encode angle \\
Output &
  Absolute over one turn &
  Incremental; an index or reference mark is needed to recover absolute
  position &
  Absolute over one turn, depending on coil layout \\
Resolution &
  12--14 bit; below 0.1$^\circ$ with interpolation &
  Set by pole pitch and interpolation; medium to high &
  Set by coil geometry; comparable to magnetic encoders with interpolation \\
Mounting &
  Requires a free shaft end centred on the sensor IC &
  Anywhere on the rim; the shaft end stays free &
  Flat PCB facing the rotor target with a controlled sub-millimetre air gap \\
Immunity to magnetic fields &
  Sensitive to stray fields from the motors and the EP connector; needs
  shielding or standoff distance &
  Same exposure as on-axis, plus sensitivity to tape bonding and alignment
  error &
  Immune; unaffected by the motors or the EP connector \\
Design effort and cost &
  Low; off-the-shelf IC with mature firmware &
  Medium; tape must be applied and aligned precisely, and a dedicated
  read-head IC is needed &
  High; custom coil design, rotor target machining and calibration, with no
  drop-in part \\
Fit for this project &
  Simplest and lowest risk, but the SMORES layout already uses the wheel shaft
  end for gearing and mounting, so a clear axial spot must be reserved &
  Frees the shaft end, but adds a homing step at power-up and a wear and
  bonding risk on the tape &
  Best fit to the EP connector's nearby field, but the highest design time and
  schedule risk within the capstone timeline \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The EP connector's field is close to the wheel sensor, so the two magnetic
options carry an interference risk the inductive option does not. Weighing
that against the extra design effort is the open decision for Phase C.

\noindent\textbf{Battery run time.} The pack is two 505080 cells in series,
3000\,mAh, 22.2\,Wh, of which 17.8\,Wh is usable to cutoff. 11.5\,W from the
pack is 1.55\,A at 7.4\,V, about 0.5\,C. Run time is about \textbf{1.5\,h}
for the full Phase D module; the Phase A and B configuration, without compute
and camera (73\,\% of the draw), runs about \textbf{5.8\,h}.

\subsubsection{Power board components}
\label{sec:power-board}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{image/Power_Diagram.png}
  \caption{Power distribution block diagram.}
  \label{fig:power-diagram}
\end{figure}

Power splits into three paths from the 2S pack:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Motors.} The four N20s run from the pack through the
        H-bridges. PWM duty is capped at about 70\,\% (6\,V over 8.4\,V at
        full charge) to hold the motors at their 6\,V rating, and the H-bridge
        current sensing trips on stall. The on-time current at stall scales to
        about 1.4\,A, so the H-bridges are rated for the pack voltage and
        1.5\,A per channel.
  \item \textbf{EP-Face and IR.} A dedicated step-down regulator feeds the
        in-wheel board and its pulse capacitor. The pulse itself comes from
        the capacitor; the separate regulator keeps the recharge current and
        pulse transients off the logic rail so the MCU and compute unit do not
        brown out during docking.
  \item \textbf{Logic and compute.} A second step-down regulator provides
        5\,V to the main board, encoder circuit, compute unit and camera;
        3.3\,V for the MCU, sensors and radios comes from an LDO on the main
        board. This rail carries about 1.6\,A typical and 4\,A peak
        (Table~\ref{tab:loads}), so the regulator is rated 5\,V / 5\,A, which
        also meets the Compute Module 5 supply requirement.
\end{itemize}
